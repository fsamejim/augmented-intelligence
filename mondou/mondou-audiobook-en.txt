Sammy: Please explain excluded middle, the P or not-P, in more detail.

Mondou: Formally: for any proposition P, either P or not-P is true. There is no third possibility. In classical logic, this is accepted as foundational. But Brouwer challenged this in 1908 from intuitionistic logic, with a fundamental objection. He argued that truth is not discovered, it is constructed. So "P is true" means you can actually construct a proof of P. "Not-P is true" means you can demonstrate that no proof of P can be constructed. And crucially, just because you haven't proven P doesn't mean you've proven not-P. There's also the problem of double negation. In classical logic, not-not-P implies P. The double negative cancels. But in intuitionistic logic, "P is not false" does not equal "P is true." They're different claims. Real-world examples of where excluded middle breaks down: vague predicates, when does a pile of sand stop being a heap? From a classical standpoint, either something exists or it doesn't, existence waits out there as a fact.
